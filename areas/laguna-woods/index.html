Laguna Woods is a small city of about 17,600 residents, according to the 2020 census, and about 90 percent of it is Laguna Woods Village. The line is open 24/7, and a leak in a manor at midnight gets the same urgency as one at noon. Same-day visits are offered when a crew is free.

Homes from the 1960s and the pipes inside them

The community that became Laguna Woods Village, formerly Leisure World, welcomed its first residents in 1964. The city incorporated on March 24, 1999 and counts about 13,400 housing units, so much of the plumbing is decades old.

Older manors can have original drain lines, aging supply pipe and water heaters in closets with tight clearance. Those are the repairs we see most often here, and each one needs the right approvals before work starts.

Approvals come before the wrench

Village guidance says the city requires a permit for every water heater replacement and that licensed contractors must do the work. Owner builders are not allowed under United Mutual, and a Mutual Consent is required before the city issues a permit.

Water and sewer service in Laguna Woods

The El Toro Water District provides all water and wastewater service in Laguna Woods. Its 2026 water quality report lists hardness of about 236 to 293 mg/L, which the USGS calls very hard, and the district can be reached at (949) 837-0660.

Hard water leaves scale on water heater elements and fixtures, which is a common reason a heater in an older manor stops working. Our water heater repair page explains the fixes.
