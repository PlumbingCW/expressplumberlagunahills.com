Nellie Gail Ranch sits in the southern part of Laguna Hills. The city's General Plan places it between Interstate 5 on the east, State Route 73 on the west and La Paz Road on the north. It covers about 1,350 acres and carries the Estate Residential designation.

What sets this neighborhood apart

The General Plan calls Nellie Gail Ranch a mixture of tract and custom homes on large lots in an equestrian setting. It counts 1,407 lots, with homes that run from about 1,700 to 17,000 square feet. The plan also lists an equestrian center, roughly 20 miles of equestrian trails, parks and a good deal of open space.

The land came out of the old Moulton Ranch. The city's history page says Lewis Moulton began that ranch in 1874 and that it was subdivided in the early 1960s. One local real estate guide says the community is named for Nellie Gail Moulton and dates from the 1970s, with a median year built around 1981, so treat those age figures as approximate.

How plumbing needs can differ on a large lot

These points are general plumbing information, not findings about any one street. A large lot usually means a longer run of pipe between the water meter and the house. It also means more outdoor plumbing, such as irrigation lines, hose bibs and pool or spa equipment.

Long runs give a hidden leak more room to hide. A rising water bill or a damp patch in the yard is worth checking early, before it turns into a bigger repair.

Large homes often have several bathrooms, more than one water heater or a long hot water line. A far fixture can take a while to deliver hot water, and every extra fixture adds shutoff valves, supply lines and traps that need to stay in good shape.

Quick checks for a large lot home

  • Read the water meter with every fixture off and see whether the leak indicator moves.
  • Walk the yard after the irrigation runs and look for soggy or unusually green patches.
  • Find the main shutoff and each fixture shutoff, and make sure they turn.
  • Look at the water heater for rust, drips on the floor or water around the base.
  • Note which drains are slow and whether more than one fixture is affected.

Water service and sewer in Laguna Hills

More than one water district serves Laguna Hills. Moulton Niguel Water District lists Laguna Hills among the six cities it serves, and El Toro Water District serves portions of the city. We could not confirm which district serves each Nellie Gail Ranch street, so check your water bill first.

Moulton Niguel Water District can be reached at (949) 831-2500 and El Toro Water District at (949) 837-0660. A district can read the meter and tell you about the main, and we handle the pipe from the house side of the meter.

Permits and your owners association

Plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The city's Building & Safety Division, at (949) 707-2600, answers permit questions for Laguna Hills addresses. A water heater replacement often needs a permit, so ask before the work starts.

Nellie Gail Ranch has an owners association. Landscape or exterior changes may need its approval, so check before anyone digs a trench to reach a buried line.
