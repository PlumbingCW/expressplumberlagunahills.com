Plumber in Aliso Meadows and Via Lomas, Laguna Hills, CA

The Via Lomas area in west central Laguna Hills is a cluster of condominiums and apartments, where a plumbing problem often touches a neighbor. Call us to talk through who handles what and what to do first.

The Via Lomas area covers about 39 acres in west central Laguna Hills. The city's General Plan counts about 350 apartments and condominiums there, including one story cluster condos and two story apartments. Aliso Meadows is the best known condominium community in the area.

What the city's plan says about this area

The General Plan describes Aliso Meadows as 248 condominiums created to offer affordable homeownership to low and moderate income households. It also names Rancho Moulton and Rancho Niguel, two apartment communities of 51 units each. The plan says they were built in the early 1980s with Section 8 construction funds as government assisted rentals.

Listing records we reviewed show Aliso Meadows condominiums dating to 1981 and sharing an association that carries the community's name. In at least one listing the association dues included water and trash, but dues differ, so read your own association documents.

Why plumbing is different in a condo or apartment

These points are general plumbing information, not findings about a specific building. In a condo or apartment, pipes, drains and ceilings are shared with neighbors above, below and beside you. A leak in one unit can reach another within hours, so the first step is to stop the water and tell the right people.

Many associations own the shared drain lines and the water supply up to each unit, while the owner is responsible for fixtures and lines inside the unit. The line between the two varies, and your association documents explain where it falls. If you rent, call your landlord or property manager first.

Water heaters in a closet

Condo water heaters often sit in a closet or utility space with little room around them. Clearance, venting and an expansion tank where the system is closed all matter when a unit is replaced. The California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets the rules.

A replacement often needs a city permit, and an association may need to approve the work. The Laguna Hills Building & Safety Division, at (949) 707-2600, answers permit questions for addresses in the city.

A note on older buildings

Age alone does not tell you what pipe is in the walls. Buildings from the early 1980s can have had repairs or upgrades over the years. We look at the actual pipe before we say what it needs.

If you see stains, hear running water in a wall or notice a musty smell, tell your association and call us. Early checks cost less than repairs after water has spread.

Water service in the area

More than one water district serves Laguna Hills, and we could not confirm which one serves each building along Via Lomas. Your water bill or your association will tell you. Moulton Niguel Water District is at (949) 831-2500 and El Toro Water District at (949) 837-0660.

Via Lomas notes: what we verified

The acreage, unit counts and building types come from the city's General Plan Land Use Element, which dates from 2009 and may not reflect later changes. Pipe materials, failure rates and shared line ownership are not documented in the sources we found.

Because ownership of shared lines depends on each association, we do not assume who pays for a repair.

Aliso Meadows and Via Lomas plumbing questions

Where is the Via Lomas area?

The General Plan places it in west central Laguna Hills, about 39 acres along Via Lomas. It includes the Aliso Meadows condominiums and uses ZIP code 92653.

Who fixes a leak that reaches my neighbor's unit?

That depends on where the leak starts and on your association documents. Stop the water, tell your association or property manager, and call us for the repair.

Do I call you or my landlord if I rent here?

Call your landlord or property manager first. They decide who does the work in a rental.

Does the association cover plumbing repairs?

It varies by association. Many cover shared lines and leave fixtures and unit lines to the owner, so read your documents.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in a condo?

Often yes. The Laguna Hills Building & Safety Division, at (949) 707-2600, can confirm what applies, and your association may need to approve it.
