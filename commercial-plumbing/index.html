What to do in an after hours plumbing emergency

A backed up restroom, a flooded kitchen floor or a failed water heater can close a business, and our 24/7 line is open for after hours calls. While you wait, stop water use in the affected area and close the nearest shutoff valve.

Same-day and after-hours visits are available when a crew is free, and weekend calls are handled the same way.

  1. Close the shutoff valve for the affected fixture or area, or the building main.
  2. Keep staff and customers away from standing water and from wet electrical equipment.
  3. Take photos for your insurer and move stock off the floor.
  4. Tell us the building type, fixture count and whether the water is wastewater or clean supply.

Common commercial plumbing calls

  • Restroom stoppages and overflowing toilets in shops and offices.
  • Kitchen line blockages in restaurants and cafes.
  • Water heater failures in shared or commercial systems.
  • Burst pipes and supply leaks in suites and storefronts.
  • Floor drain backups in back of house areas.
  • Fixture repair in multi unit and shared buildings.

The first visit stabilizes the problem. We then explain the cause, the repair and anything that should be scheduled for a quieter hour.

Kitchen lines and grease

Restaurant drains carry grease that cools and coats the pipe. A cable opens the line, but hardened grease often returns quickly. Hydro jetting cleans the full wall, and a camera shows whether a break or belly is collecting waste.

Grease traps and interceptors collect fat before it reaches the sewer. They need regular pumping and cleaning, and the interval depends on kitchen volume and the trap size. Your local sewer agency and the Orange County Health Care Agency set the rules that apply to a food facility.

Backflow testing

A backflow assembly protects the public water supply from contamination. Water districts require periodic testing, usually once a year, and a certified tester files the result.

Tell us the assembly type and your water district when you call. The El Toro Water District can be reached at (949) 837-0660 and Moulton Niguel Water District at (949) 831-2500.

Businesses also call us for hidden leaks. A water bill that climbs with no change in use, or a meter that moves after closing time, is the sign, and we test the supply line before any floor is opened.

Storm drains and site drainage

Parking lots, loading docks and planters feed storm drains, and debris blocks them quickly. Clearing a storm drain before rain protects the property, and a camera shows whether a pipe is damaged.
