When a toilet problem is an emergency

A toilet that overflows, leaks from the base or will not stop filling is an emergency, and our line is open 24/7. While you wait, turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water.

Same-day and after-hours visits are available when a crew is free, and stopping the water first limits the damage to the floor below.

  1. Turn the oval handle on the supply line behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  2. If the bowl is rising, lift the lid of the tank and push the flapper down to stop the fill.
  3. Do not flush again. Use towels to keep water off wood, carpet and cabinets.
  4. If more than one fixture is backing up, stop all water use, since the problem may be in the main line.

Many toilet repairs finish in one visit. If the porcelain is cracked or the flange is broken, a new toilet or a flange repair is the honest answer.

Clogs: when a plunger is enough

A flat bottom plunger with a firm seal clears many toilet clogs. If it does not work after a few tries, an auger made for toilets reaches the trap. Avoid chemical cleaners in a bowl that is already full of water.

A toilet that clogs again and again may have a drain line problem. In that case drain cleaning or a camera check looks beyond the bowl.

Replacing or installing a toilet

A new toilet is set on a wax ring or a waxless seal over the floor flange, then bolted down and connected to the supply. The flange must be sound and at the right height, or the seal can leak.

  • Confirm the rough in distance from the wall, commonly 12 inches.
  • Check the flange for cracks, corrosion or a low position.
  • Use a new supply line and shutoff valve if the old ones show wear.
  • Choose a high efficiency model that meets current flow limits.

Permits for toilet work

The City of Laguna Hills publishes a handout of common projects exempt from permits, and it lists faucet or toilet repair or replacement as exempt. That covers like for like swaps and repairs, not moving a drain.

Laguna Woods Village is stricter. Its guidance says toilets, sinks and faucets need a city permit, so tell us if your home is in Laguna Woods. Work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

What a toilet visit includes

  1. Stop the water

    We shut the supply and protect the floor.

  2. Diagnose

    We check the tank, the seal, the flange and the drain.

  3. Repair or replace

    We fix the part or set the new toilet, using new seals and supply lines.

  4. Test

    We flush several times and check for leaks around the base and the supply.

We give a clear price before work starts, and the price depends on the parts, the flange and the hour of the visit.

Toilet Repair questions, answered

Do you handle toilet emergencies at night or on weekends in Laguna Hills?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. Close the supply valve behind the toilet, then call, and we book a visit as soon as a crew is available.

Why does my toilet keep running?

Most often the flapper no longer seals or the fill valve is worn. Both are quick repairs.

Do I need a permit to replace a toilet?

The Laguna Hills handout of common projects exempt from permits lists toilet repair or replacement. Laguna Woods Village follows stricter guidance, so ask before you start.

Can you fix a leak at the base of the toilet?

Yes. It is usually a failed wax ring or a loose flange, and we reset the toilet with a new seal.

Will you remove the old toilet?

Yes. Removal and disposal are part of a replacement, and we explain the price before we start.
