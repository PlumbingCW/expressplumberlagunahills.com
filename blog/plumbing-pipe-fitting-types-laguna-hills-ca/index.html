Plumbing Pipe Fitting Types for Laguna Hills, CA Homes

Fittings connect pipes, change direction and control flow. This guide explains the common types, which materials they pair with and what the code says in Laguna Hills.

By the Laguna Hills Express Plumbing team. Published October 17, 2026.

Call (949) 325-3122 now.

Every plumbing system is pipe and fittings. Pipe carries water or waste, and fittings join the pieces, turn corners, split a line or close it. Choosing the wrong fitting is a common reason for leaks.

This guide groups fittings by the job they do. It also notes which materials they pair with, since a fitting made for copper does not work on PEX.

Fittings that change direction

Elbows turn a line, most often by 90 or 45 degrees. A long sweep elbow turns more gently, which helps drain lines flow. A short elbow suits tight supply runs.

On drain lines the turn matters. A sharp turn can catch solids, so a sweep or two 45 degree elbows are preferred where the code asks for them.

Fittings that join and split

Common joining and branching fittings
FittingWhat it doesTypical use
CouplingJoins two pipes end to endRepairing a cut section.
UnionJoins two pipes and can be taken apartWater heater and pump connections.
TeeSplits a line into a branchFeeding a fixture off a main run.
WyeBranches a drain at an angleDrain lines, so waste flows smoothly.
ReducerJoins two pipes of different sizesStepping down to a fixture line.
CapCloses the end of a pipeSealing an abandoned line.

A union is a service friendly choice because it allows the connection to be opened without cutting the pipe.

Valves and controls

  • Ball valves quarter turn to open or close and are common on main lines.
  • Gate valves use a wheel and are found on older homes. They often fail to seal.
  • Angle stops sit under sinks and toilets and shut off one fixture.
  • Check valves allow flow in one direction.
  • Pressure regulators lower incoming pressure to a safe level.

Old valves that have not moved in years often leak or seize when turned. Replace them during other work when you can.

Matching fittings to pipe materials

Which fittings pair with which pipe
PipeTypical fittingsJoining method
CopperSweat fittings, press fittingsSolder or press tool.
PEXCrimp, clamp or expansion fittingsCrimp ring, clamp or expansion.
PVC and ABS drain pipeSocket fittingsSolvent cement.
Cast ironNo hub couplings or hub fittingsClamps or lead and oakum in older work.
Black iron gas pipeThreaded fittingsThreaded with sealant rated for gas.

Mixing metals can cause corrosion. A dielectric union is used when copper meets galvanized steel.

Sizes, labels and what they mean

Pipe sizes use a nominal number that does not match the real measurement. A half inch copper line has an outside diameter of five eighths of an inch, and a fitting must match the pipe type, not just the number. Labels stamped on a fitting show the size, the material and sometimes the pressure rating.

Push to connect fittings are popular for quick repairs because they need no solder or tools. They work on clean, round pipe, but they cost more than soldered or crimped joints, and some plumbers prefer not to bury them in walls.

What to do before you start a fitting repair

  • Shut off the water at the nearest valve, or at the main if you cannot find one.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line.
  • Wipe the pipe dry and check it for corrosion past the fitting.
  • Match the new fitting to the pipe material and size.
  • Turn the water on slowly and check each joint.

Keep a few spare fittings that match your plumbing, such as a coupling and a shutoff valve. A small kit can turn a weekend leak into a ten minute repair while you wait for a plumber.

Choosing fittings for a repair

Match the fitting to the pipe in your wall, not to the pipe you wish you had. Copper, PEX, CPVC and galvanized steel each have their own fittings and transition parts. A transition fitting joins two materials safely, and it is the right answer when a repair touches two different pipes.

For a repair behind a wall, many plumbers use a press or crimp fitting. For an exposed repair, a union or a shutoff valve makes future work easier.

Laguna Hills notes: the code your fittings must meet

Plumbing in Laguna Hills follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The city's Building & Safety Division applies the 2025 California Building Standards Code to applications filed on or after January 1, 2026, and it can be reached at (949) 707-2600.

The city also runs an online Self Service Permit Portal for permit applications. Like for like faucet or toilet repairs are listed as exempt from permits, but new lines and fittings generally are not.

Sources: City of Laguna Hills Building & Safety Division, California Building Standards Commission.

When fittings are a clue to a bigger problem

Green crust on a copper fitting means a slow leak or corrosion. White crust is mineral scale from hard water. Both are signs to look at the line, not just wipe the fitting.

If you see them across the house, a repiping assessment may save repeat repairs. For a single fitting that weeps, leak detection can confirm the leak is only one fitting.

The same fitting types apply in Laguna Niguel and Laguna Woods, although each city has its own permit office.

Local questions, answered

What is the difference between a coupling and a union?

A coupling joins two pipes permanently. A union can be unscrewed, so equipment can be removed without cutting pipe.

Can I mix copper and PEX in the same system?

Yes, with the right transition fittings. The connection point must use fittings made for both materials.

Do I need a permit to replace a fitting in Laguna Hills?

A single like for like repair often does not. New lines and larger replacements generally do, so ask the Building & Safety Division at (949) 707-2600.

Why do old valves leak when turned?

The packing and seats dry out after years without moving. Replacing them during other work avoids a leak later.
