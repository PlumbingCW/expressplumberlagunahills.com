Detecting Water Leaks Underground in Laguna Hills, CA

A leak in a buried water line can run for months without a visible puddle. This guide shows simple tests you can do at home and how a plumber locates the leak.

By the Laguna Hills Express Plumbing team. Published October 14, 2026.

Call (949) 325-3122 now.

Underground leaks hide because soil absorbs water. The first sign is often your bill, not a puddle. By the time a lawn goes soft, the leak may have been running for weeks.

The good news is that a few simple checks tell you whether a leak exists before you call anyone.

Signs of a buried leak

  • A water bill that rises with no change in how you live.
  • A lawn patch that is green, soft or sunken while the rest is normal.
  • Low pressure at every faucet in the house.
  • The sound of running water when everything is off.
  • A meter box that is full of water.
  • Warm water in the ground over a hot water line.

A meter test you can do in two minutes

  1. Turn off

    Shut off every tap, appliance and irrigation system that uses water.

  2. Read

    Open the meter box and look at the small leak indicator or the dial.

  3. Wait

    Check again after fifteen minutes without using any water.

  4. Compare

    If the indicator spins or the numbers rose, water is leaking somewhere.

To tell if the leak is before or after the house shutoff, close the main valve at the house and check the meter again. If it still moves, the leak is in the buried line between the meter and the house.

How a plumber locates the leak

A pressure test confirms that the line is losing water. Acoustic equipment then listens along the pipe route for the hiss of escaping water. A ground microphone on the surface finds the loudest point, and a correlator can pinpoint the leak between two contact points.

Leak location follows industry practice for pressure testing, and repairs follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as California adopts it. Once the spot is found, a small excavation exposes the pipe. That keeps digging, and the repair to your yard, as small as possible. Our leak detection service uses this method.

Irrigation leaks versus service line leaks

A yard has more than one water line. The service line brings water from the meter to the house, and the irrigation system has its own pipes and valves. A wet patch near a sprinkler head is more likely an irrigation leak, and a wet patch along the path from the meter to the house is more likely the service line.

To separate them, close the irrigation shutoff or the irrigation backflow valve, then repeat the meter test. If the meter stops, the leak was in the irrigation system. If it keeps moving, the service line or the house plumbing is losing water.

Write down the meter reading before bed and again in the morning. If no one used water overnight and the number changed, a leak is running while you sleep.

How long a leak can run before it shows

A small underground leak can run for months. Soil absorbs the water, and the pipe may sit deep enough that nothing reaches the surface. A leak of only a few gallons an hour adds up to a large volume over a month.

That is why bills matter. Compare this month's use to the same month last year, and call your water district if the difference has no explanation. Early detection costs less than a repair that follows months of soil washout.

What to do while you wait

What to do before the leak locator arrives

  • Close the main valve at the house if water is visible.
  • Mark the soft or wet spot with a flag or stake.
  • Take a photo of the meter reading and the time.
  • Do not dig. Buried gas and electric lines may be close by.
  • Keep children and pets off soft ground.

If the line serves a home in Laguna Niguel or Laguna Woods, tell the dispatcher so the correct district is contacted.

Local questions, answered

How do I find out if my leak is in the yard or the house?

Close the main valve at the house and check the meter. If it still moves, the leak is in the buried line before the shutoff.

Who do I call for a leak on the street side of the meter in Laguna Hills?

Call your water district. The El Toro Water District is at (949) 837-0660 and Moulton Niguel Water District is at (949) 831-2500. Your bill shows which one serves you.

Will detection tear up my yard?

No. Locating the leak uses listening equipment above ground. Digging happens only at the spot where the leak is found.

Can a leak cause a sinkhole?

Water leaving a pipe can wash soil away over time. Soft or sinking ground is a reason to call quickly and to stay off the area.
