What Is Venting in Plumbing? A Laguna Hills, CA Homeowner Guide

Vents are the pipes you never see, but your drains depend on them. This guide explains what plumbing vents do, how they fail and how they differ from a water heater flue.

By the Laguna Hills Express Plumbing team. Published October 26, 2026.

Call (949) 325-3122 now.

A plumbing vent is a pipe that connects the drain system to the outside air, usually through the roof. It does two jobs. It lets air in behind flowing water, and it lets sewer gas out of the system.

Without air, draining water pulls a vacuum behind it. The result is a slow drain, a gurgle and a trap that loses its water seal.

How a vent works

Think of a bottle turned upside down. If the bottle has one small hole, water glugs out. If air can enter another way, water pours smoothly. A vent plays that role for every fixture.

The vent pipe runs from each trap arm to a vertical stack that goes through the roof. Some fixtures share a vent. Others use an air admittance valve, a one way device that opens to let air in when needed.

Vent types you may see
TypeHow it worksWhere it is used
Stack ventThe upper part of the main stack that extends through the roofMost homes.
Branch ventA line that serves a group of fixturesBathrooms and kitchens.
Wet ventA pipe that vents one fixture and drains anotherAllowed in some layouts.
Air admittance valveA one way valve that lets air inIslands and remodel areas where code allows it.

Signs a vent is blocked

  • Gurgling from a drain after another fixture runs.
  • Slow draining that clearing the trap does not fix.
  • A sewer smell in the bathroom or kitchen.
  • Toilets that bubble when the shower drains.
  • A trap that keeps losing its water.

Vent size and layout follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as California adopts it. Leaves, nests and debris are the usual blockages. Roots do not enter vents, but a vent pipe can crack or be crushed in an attic.

Checking a vent safely

A plumber can run a camera or a cable from the roof opening to clear a blocked vent. Climbing onto a roof is dangerous, so do not attempt it unless you are comfortable and have fall protection.

From the ground, look for vent pipes on the roof and check that they are open and not covered. A missing cap or a hole is a sign of a problem.

Plumbing vents are not water heater vents

A gas water heater has a separate flue that carries combustion gases outside. It has its own rules for height, slope and clearance. A plumbing vent carries air and sewer gas, and it never connects to a water heater flue.

Venting problems after a remodel

A remodel can break a vent without anyone noticing. A new wall can cover a vent, a relocated sink can leave its trap too far from the vent, and a patched roof can seal a vent opening. The first sign is often a drain that gurgles for the first time.

If the symptoms started after construction, tell the plumber what changed. That narrows the search and can save time on the first visit.

Why vents end above the roof

A vent that ends at the roof carries sewer gas above the house, where it disperses. The opening must sit clear of windows, doors and air intakes, so gas does not drift back inside. The code sets minimum heights and distances for these terminations.

In cold climates a vent can frost over. In Laguna Hills that is rare, and the usual vent blockage here is debris from trees or a nest. A plumber can clear it from the roof opening.

Venting and a clogged drain are different problems

A clog stops water. A vent problem slows water and makes noise. If a drain is slow and gurgles at the same time, both can be present, and the plumber checks the vent as well as the line.

Laguna Hills and Laguna Niguel notes: water heater flue rules

The City of Laguna Hills water heater handout says the horizontal run of a vent may not exceed 75 percent of its vertical height. It also covers combustion air, a drain pan and a temperature and pressure relief valve.

Laguna Niguel's handout adds that gas vents slope upward at one quarter inch per foot. The city's Building Division can be reached at (949) 362-4360, and Laguna Hills Building & Safety at (949) 707-2600.

Sources: City of Laguna Hills water heater handout, City of Laguna Niguel water heater handout.

When to call

Call a plumber if you notice

  • Gurgling or slow drains in more than one fixture.
  • A sewer smell that does not clear with water in the traps.
  • A visible crack or gap in an attic vent pipe.
  • A new vent added during a remodel that you want checked.
  • A water heater that backdrafts or smells of combustion.

For a gas water heater, see water heater installation for venting requirements. Houses in Laguna Woods and Laguna Niguel have their own permit offices for such work.

Local questions, answered

Why do my drains gurgle?

Gurgling means air is being pulled through the water in a trap. A blocked vent is one cause. A partial clog is another.

Can I add a vent myself?

Venting has strict code rules. A new or changed vent should be planned by a plumber and inspected where required.

What are the water heater vent rules in Laguna Hills?

The city's handout says the horizontal run may not exceed 75 percent of the vertical height. Ask the Building & Safety Division at (949) 707-2600 for details.

What is an air admittance valve?

It is a one way valve that opens to let air into a drain line. Some codes allow it where a roof vent is hard to run.
